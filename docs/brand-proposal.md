# Bear Grid Visual Identity Proposal

**Status:** Strategic proposal only — no artwork approved, no website implementation authorised
**Prepared for:** Bear Grid
**Brand role:** Independent European engineering and business consultancy
**Architecture constraint:** Preserve the current website positioning, page architecture, navigation, content hierarchy and preserved original-platform archive

---

## 1. Executive recommendation

Bear Grid already has the beginning of the right visual posture: quiet colours, direct language, a rigorous page grid and very little decorative noise. The problem is not a lack of “design.” It is a lack of a distinctive, coherent identity that makes those choices unmistakably Bear Grid.

The strongest route is **Direction 1 — Engineered Quiet**. It combines Swiss editorial discipline with the physical character of European engineering documents: warm paper, graphite ink, precise rules, measured typography and one controlled copper signal. It feels senior and technically literate without resembling a software product, a creative agency or a nostalgic industrial brand.

Recommended identity combination:

- **Direction:** Engineered Quiet
- **Primary logo route:** BG Datum monogram, paired with a custom Bear Grid wordmark
- **Secondary heritage device:** Refined bear mark, used sparingly
- **Typography:** IBM Plex Sans + IBM Plex Mono
- **Signature colour:** Oxide copper, used as an index or datum—not a marketing accent
- **Image language:** Real systems, materials, sites, teams and evidence; no stock illustration
- **UI posture:** Flat, ruled, square and calm; hierarchy comes from type, spacing and alignment

Nothing in this proposal requires a change to Bear Grid’s current positioning or information architecture. The identity should strengthen the existing consultancy story, not reinvent it.

---

## 2. Brand principles

The future identity should pass five tests.

1. **Credible before expressive.** It should look appropriate on a technical report, investment memo, workshop wall and project handover—not only on a homepage.
2. **Distinctive through restraint.** Recognition should come from typography, a precise symbol, grid logic and disciplined colour use rather than visual effects.
3. **European, not generically global-tech.** The tone should feel measured, materially aware and editorial, with references to engineering practice and Swiss information design.
4. **Senior and direct.** The system should imply that clients work with experienced people who diagnose, decide and deliver.
5. **Built to last.** Avoid fashion-dependent gradients, inflated display type, glass effects, mascot illustration and decorative UI.

### Verbal-to-visual translation

| Existing positioning | Required visual expression |
| --- | --- |
| Independent consultancy | Unbranded confidence, modest scale, no venture-style theatre |
| Engineering + business | Technical precision balanced by clear, readable editorial typography |
| Solve hard problems | Structured information, visible logic, purposeful diagrams |
| Small senior teams | Human evidence and direct photography, not corporate stock scenes |
| Build and deliver | Material detail, working artefacts, plans, prototypes and systems |
| Dutch / European context | Quiet modernism, practical colour, strong grid, generous but controlled space |

# Phase 1 — Current identity review

## 3. Audit scope and context

The review covers the current consultancy site at its primary routes, including the homepage, expertise, investors, history, contact, legal layer and shared header/footer. The original Bear Grid sensing platform is treated as a preserved historical archive. Its legacy visual language is valuable evidence of the company’s origin, but it should not define the consultancy identity.

The current site’s architecture and positioning are strong enough to preserve:

- Direct consultancy proposition
- Clear service, process, audience, history, ecosystem, FAQ and contact sequence
- Separate current-company and original-platform experiences
- Editorial grid and restrained component count
- Straightforward calls to action

The redesign should be a system-level visual refinement, not a content or architecture rewrite.

## 4. Current strengths

- The warm off-white and charcoal palette is more credible than a conventional white-and-blue consultancy site.
- The site uses borders and alignment rather than cards and effects to create structure.
- The language is direct, practical and senior.
- The current section numbering gives the site useful document-like logic.
- Large areas of negative space create confidence when proportioned correctly.
- The dark process and closing sections provide clear tonal punctuation.
- Forms are simple, legible and free from unnecessary iconography.
- The ecosystem section is framed as community context rather than inflated social proof.
- The responsive layouts retain the hierarchy without turning the mobile site into a separate visual product.
- Reduced-motion support and semantic UI patterns are already present.

## 5. Weaknesses by identity element

### Logo

The current bear-head mark is recognisable as a bear but reads as a small mascot or app icon rather than a precision identity for an engineering consultancy. Its facial detail collapses at navigation size, its silhouette is visually soft, and its style is disconnected from the strict grid used elsewhere.

The separate “BEAR GRID” text appears to be ordinary bold interface type rather than a designed wordmark. The relationship between mark and name feels assembled rather than authored.

**Consequence:** The brand’s most ownable asset is also its least premium and least technically credible element.

### Favicon

The current favicon uses a white **B** inside a rounded black square, while the site header uses a bear head. This creates two competing identity systems. The rounded-square construction also leans toward an app or SaaS product.

**Consequence:** Recognition is inconsistent across tabs, bookmarks, devices and the website.

### Typography

Inter is highly functional, but in this context it contributes to a familiar Linear-style technology aesthetic. The very large headlines, extremely tight tracking and low line heights intensify that association. Smaller labels use tracked uppercase effectively, but the system lacks a second typographic voice for evidence, specifications, dates and technical data.

At mobile sizes, display headlines become visually dominant enough to make the site feel like a design-led studio rather than a measured engineering consultancy.

**Consequence:** Strong impact, but limited distinctiveness and an occasionally fashionable/startup tone.

### Spacing

The site is generous, but the spacing is not always visibly governed by one scale. Some sections use excellent measured intervals; others create very large empty areas, especially where partner assets do not establish enough visual mass. Dense service content and expansive feature sections can feel like two different systems.

**Consequence:** The rhythm sometimes reads as “large because premium” rather than deliberately calibrated.

### Colour palette

The existing palette—off-white, light warm grey, mid-grey and charcoal—is appropriate but nearly generic. There is no distinctive brand signal beyond monochrome. The green WhatsApp control introduces a strong external colour, while error, consent and archive states operate as isolated exceptions.

**Consequence:** Sophisticated neutrality without strong recall.

### Buttons

Square, bordered buttons are directionally correct. However, the button hierarchy is underdeveloped: primary, secondary, text and utility actions do not yet feel like members of one exact system. Labels such as “Let’s Talk” are small inside large editorial compositions, and the standalone WhatsApp pill introduces a rounded, shadowed visual language that conflicts with the rest of the interface.

**Consequence:** Actions work, but they do not consistently reinforce the brand.

### Links

Navigation underline motion and text underlines are clear, but link behaviour varies between animated lines, permanent rules and arrow-based service CTAs. The arrow is generic and sometimes adds noise where a typographic cue would suffice.

**Consequence:** Interaction is understandable but not fully systematised.

### Forms

The form has a useful technical simplicity. Its line-only fields, however, can resemble a template and make field boundaries less obvious for some users. Labels, hints, errors and completion states need a stronger state system. The warm panel is appropriate but lacks distinctive brand logic.

**Consequence:** Functional and restrained, with room for stronger accessibility and precision.

### Cards and lists

The site correctly avoids excessive floating cards. Service and process modules rely on ruled grids, which is a strength. Yet each list type has slightly different spacing, numbering, border and typographic logic.

**Consequence:** The page feels coherent at a glance but less consistent under close inspection.

### Icons

The current consultancy experience mostly avoids icons, which is correct. The exceptions—bear mark, WhatsApp glyph, menu lines and plus/minus disclosure symbols—do not share a common stroke or construction logic.

**Consequence:** A small issue today, but one that would grow as the system expands.

### Illustrations and diagrams

The current consultancy site uses very little proprietary diagram language. The preserved original-platform materials include useful system diagrams, but their illustrative style belongs to an earlier product chapter.

**Consequence:** Bear Grid’s analytical and engineering capability is stated in words more often than it is demonstrated visually.

### Partner / ecosystem section

The restrained grid is appropriate, and the disclosure language is responsible. The partner marks vary significantly in colour, proportions, resolution and visual weight. In the current long-page composition, the section can produce a disproportionate amount of empty space or inconsistent emphasis.

**Consequence:** The section can look unfinished or more promotional than intended, despite careful wording.

### Imagery and photography

The main consultancy experience has almost no photography. This avoids stock imagery but also removes evidence of people, materials, places, workshops, systems and work in progress. The brand risks feeling abstract.

**Consequence:** High conceptual clarity, low tangible proof.

### Page rhythm

The homepage has a sound macro-sequence: proposition → problems → services → method → audience → philosophy → ecosystem → FAQ → CTA. The repeated formula of eyebrow plus oversized heading becomes predictable, while the alternation between very dense and very open sections sometimes lacks an intermediate pace.

**Consequence:** Strong architecture with insufficient visual modulation.

### Consistency

The current company site, legal pages, floating utilities and preserved original platform each carry slightly different conventions. Some separation is intentional, but shared brand elements—logo, favicon, type, focus states and legal/utility controls—should feel related.

**Consequence:** The architecture is coherent, but the total brand experience is not yet seamless.

### Accessibility

Positive foundations include semantic structure, responsive layouts, reduced-motion support, visible text labels and clear content order.

Priority improvements for the eventual system:

- Define colour pairs that meet WCAG 2.2 AA at minimum.
- Do not rely on colour alone for state or emphasis.
- Use a consistent, high-contrast focus ring with at least 2 px visual weight.
- Retain 44 × 44 px minimum interactive targets.
- Increase the size and contrast of 10–11 px eyebrow and metadata text where it carries meaning.
- Give form fields persistent boundaries or backgrounds, not only a bottom rule.
- Preserve readable line heights when using condensed or tightly tracked display type.
- Keep motion optional and non-essential.
- Provide clear accessible labels for all marks and partner links.

### Premium perception

The site currently feels considered and modern, but not yet proprietary. Its strongest traits—large sans-serif headlines, neutral surfaces, border grids and small uppercase labels—are widely used by technology studios and design agencies.

To become premium in the right way, Bear Grid needs:

- A symbol with construction logic and small-size performance
- More measured headline scale
- A typographic pairing with technical authority
- One ownable colour signal
- A disciplined image and diagram system
- Tighter component consistency
- Better control of partner marks and utility UI

Premium should mean **specific, calm and exact**, not larger, darker or more luxurious.

# Phase 2 — Three identity directions

## 6. Direction 1 — Engineered Quiet

### Concept

A contemporary European engineering identity built from datum lines, measured spacing, warm technical paper and one oxide-copper signal. It combines the clarity of Swiss information design with the practical character of drawings, field notes, manufacturing documentation and decision memos.

This is the recommended direction.

### Personality

Precise, calm, senior, useful, assured, material, independent.

### Colour palette

| Role | Name | HEX | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Primary background | Drafting Paper | `#F2F0E9` | Main canvas |
| Raised background | Chalk | `#FAF9F5` | Forms, selected panels, image mats |
| Primary text | Graphite | `#191A18` | Headlines, body, primary controls |
| Secondary text | Iron | `#565852` | Supporting copy, captions |
| Rules | Aluminium | `#B8B8B0` | Borders, dividers, diagrams |
| Strong rules | Gunmetal | `#6D706A` | Form borders, active rules |
| Signature | Oxide Copper | `#A64B2A` | Datum points, active index, selected state |
| Dark field | Furnace | `#22231F` | Footer, process, technical inversions |
| Success | Workshop Green | `#2F684C` | Confirmations only |
| Error | Signal Red | `#A33A32` | Errors only |

Copper is limited to roughly 5% of any composition. It should behave like an engineer’s annotation—not a marketing highlight.

### Typography

- **Primary:** IBM Plex Sans, variable where available
- **Technical:** IBM Plex Mono
- **Fallbacks:** Arial / system sans and system monospace

Rationale: IBM Plex has an engineered, human quality and a broad weight range without feeling trendy. It handles interface copy and serious editorial text. Plex Mono gives dates, labels, measurements and process information a genuine technical voice.

Display typography should be smaller and more deliberate than the current system:

- No headline tighter than `-0.035em`
- No display line height below `0.94`
- Prefer 2–4 balanced lines over oversized one-word breaks
- Sentence case by default
- Uppercase reserved for metadata and drawing-style labels

### Logo direction

A custom **BG Datum** monogram: the vertical of the B and the horizontal of the G share one construction line. A single square datum point in oxide copper identifies the grid intersection. Paired with a spaced but not widely tracked “Bear Grid” wordmark in a customised Plex-derived grotesk.

The bear remains available as a secondary heritage mark, reduced to a strict, geometric silhouette.

### Favicon concept

The BG Datum monogram inside no container. At 16 px it becomes a black stepped form with one optional copper pixel. For monochrome contexts, the datum point is expressed as negative space.

### Icon style

Icons are rare and functional:

- 1.5 px optical stroke at 24 px
- Square terminals
- No filled circles unless they indicate a status or datum
- Built on a 4 px sub-grid
- No decorative containers
- Text labels remain visible for important actions

### Button style

- Square or 2 px radius
- 1 px graphite border
- Primary: graphite fill, paper text
- Secondary: transparent, graphite border
- Tertiary: text plus a short copper datum rule
- Hover: colour inversion or 2 px directional inset rule; no lift
- Focus: 2 px copper outline with 2 px offset
- Labels use 14–15 px medium weight, sentence case

### Cards

No floating cards. Use **ruled modules**:

- 1 px shared borders
- Number or technical label aligned to the top-left datum
- Content aligned to one internal baseline grid
- Optional copper index line, never a full coloured border
- No drop shadows

### Links

Body links use a persistent underline with a 3 px offset. On hover, the underline changes from graphite to copper. Navigation uses a short datum mark aligned to the first letter rather than a full animated underline. External links receive a text suffix only where necessary.

### Forms

- Chalk panel on Drafting Paper
- Fully bounded 1 px fields with a 2 px radius
- 14 px persistent labels above inputs
- 16 px input text
- Technical helper copy in Plex Mono at 12–13 px
- Copper focus border plus an external outline
- Errors combine icon-free text, red rule and explicit error message
- One-column form rhythm retained; no decorative steps

### Illustration and imagery style

Create proprietary **evidence diagrams**:

- Black and aluminium linework on paper
- Oxide copper for the critical path, constraint or decision
- Axes, datum marks, captions and numbered callouts
- Isometric views only when spatial understanding requires them
- No faux-AI nodes, glowing networks or generic dashboards
- Existing original-platform diagrams remain in the archive and can be presented as historical artefacts on neutral mats

### Photography style

- Real European industrial, technical and working environments
- Natural or overcast light, honest material colour
- People shown working, reviewing, measuring or making—not smiling at camera
- Crops emphasise hands, tools, documents, interfaces, prototypes and site context
- Occasional wide environmental frame paired with close evidence detail
- No blue overlays, dramatic teal/orange grade or staged meeting-room stock

### Motion principles

- Functional, under 220 ms for UI transitions
- Mostly opacity, rule extension and small 4–8 px translation
- Content enters by row or datum, not by floating cards
- No parallax, elastic easing or decorative cursor effects
- Respect reduced motion
- Diagrams may reveal a process path once, then remain static

### Overall feeling

An engineering practice that communicates as clearly as it works: quiet, exact and physically grounded.

### Why it matches Bear Grid

Bear Grid’s proposition is not “innovation” in the abstract; it is solving difficult work through judgement and delivery. Engineered Quiet gives that promise a visible operating system. It preserves the current site’s editorial restraint while replacing generic technology cues with a proprietary technical language. The oxide datum provides recognition without turning the brand into a colourful product identity.

## 7. Direction 2 — Civic Modern

### Concept

A public-infrastructure and architectural identity: rational, highly legible and quietly authoritative. It draws from Dutch wayfinding, civic publications, architectural practices and standards manuals rather than software interfaces.

### Personality

Open, dependable, intelligent, public-minded, clear, continental, durable.

### Colour palette

| Role | Name | HEX | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Primary background | Mineral White | `#F7F6F1` | Main canvas |
| Secondary background | Limestone | `#E7E3DA` | Alternating bands |
| Primary text | Near Black | `#171917` | Text and controls |
| Secondary text | Slate | `#4E5553` | Supporting text |
| Rules | Concrete | `#B9BBB5` | Lines and fields |
| Signature | Civic Green | `#245B4A` | Navigation, key headings, active state |
| Secondary accent | Brick | `#9A4035` | Rare annotation and alert |
| Dark field | Deep Green | `#173B32` | Footer and key institutional panels |
| Success | Clear Green | `#256647` | Confirmations |
| Error | Brick Red | `#9A332D` | Errors |

The palette is institutional rather than lifestyle-led. Green is deep and muted, never gradient-based.

### Typography

- **Primary:** Source Sans 3
- **Editorial / quotations:** Source Serif 4
- **Technical fallback:** system monospace used only for tables and measurements

Rationale: Source Sans is exceptionally legible and less product-coded than Inter. Source Serif adds authority to key statements, quotations and historical material without making the identity traditional.

### Logo direction

A **Grid Bear combination mark**. The bear is not illustrated literally; its head and shoulders emerge from a nine-cell grid through selected filled modules and negative space. The wordmark is sentence case, with slightly compact proportions and a custom diagonal cut in the G.

### Favicon concept

A simplified four-cell version of the grid mark: two ears, central negative muzzle and a stable square base. It remains recognisable in one colour and does not require a rounded container.

### Icon style

- Wayfinding-inspired
- 2 px stroke at 24 px
- Geometric but humanised corners
- Solid arrowheads
- High-recognition forms
- Used only for navigation utilities, disclosures and statuses

### Button style

- 3 px radius
- Deep green primary fill
- Mineral white text
- Secondary is white with a 1 px green border
- Generous horizontal padding, moderate height
- Hover darkens without movement
- Focus uses a 3 px high-contrast outer ring
- Labels remain sentence case

### Cards

Cards are **information plates**:

- Limestone fill or white fill with 1 px concrete border
- 3 px radius
- Clear header/body separation
- Small route or category label above the title
- No shadow by default
- Selected or featured plate uses a 4 px civic-green top rule

### Links

Underlined by default in body copy. Navigation uses a compact green block or square before the active item. Descriptive links use explicit language rather than arrows alone.

### Forms

- White fields within a limestone panel
- Persistent 1 px boundary and 3 px radius
- 48–52 px field height
- Clear required and optional language
- Deep-green focus ring
- Helper and validation copy placed immediately below the relevant field
- Submit state includes clear text; no spinner-only state

### Illustration and imagery style

- Architectural elevations
- Process maps
- Stakeholder and system diagrams
- Colour-blocked plans with green as the main path and brick as risk/exception
- Restrained legends and plain-language captions
- Occasional serif pull quote or project principle

### Photography style

- European streets, buildings, workshops, test sites and infrastructure
- Wide, stable perspectives with level horizons
- Human presence is contextual rather than performative
- Soft daylight and realistic colour
- Photography may be paired with factual captions, place and year

### Motion principles

- Resembles physical wayfinding: reveal, slide, replace
- 160–240 ms, standard ease-out
- Active navigation marker moves in a straight path
- Accordion and form transitions prioritise spatial clarity
- No continuous ambient animation

### Overall feeling

A trusted independent practice that could advise a founder, an industrial company, an investor or a public institution with equal credibility.

### Why it matches Bear Grid

Bear Grid operates between technology, business and real-world execution. Civic Modern communicates breadth and trust without looking corporate. It gives the Dutch and European context greater visibility and makes complex information accessible. It is warmer and more public-facing than Engineered Quiet, with slightly less technical edge.

## 8. Direction 3 — Field Standard

### Concept

A robust identity inspired by field equipment, calibration labels, technical cases, inspection reports and operating standards. It is the most industrial of the three directions, but it avoids militaristic, gaming or retro-workwear cues.

### Personality

Capable, direct, compact, resilient, pragmatic, decisive, technical.

### Colour palette

| Role | Name | HEX | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Primary background | Calibration Grey | `#ECEDE8` | Main canvas |
| Secondary background | Equipment White | `#F8F8F4` | Reading surfaces |
| Primary text | Carbon | `#151716` | Text, marks, controls |
| Secondary text | Steel | `#505653` | Supporting copy |
| Rules | Zinc | `#A7ACA7` | Dividers, grid |
| Signature | Safety Ochre | `#D08A16` | Active label, callout, selected state |
| Secondary accent | Deep Teal | `#174F50` | Technical category, dark field |
| Dark field | Carbon Black | `#151716` | Footer, process blocks |
| Success | Verified Green | `#276343` | Confirmations |
| Error | Stop Red | `#A6312D` | Errors |

Ochre is used as a calibrated signal colour, not as a broad lifestyle wash.

### Typography

- **Primary:** Archivo
- **Technical / labels:** Roboto Mono
- **Optional condensed display:** Archivo Narrow, limited to diagrams, indexes and large section numbers

Rationale: Archivo was designed for high-performance typography and works well across display and interface roles. Its industrial tone is more distinctive than Inter while remaining contemporary and accessible.

### Logo direction

An **Abstract Grid / Field Plate** symbol made from two interlocking brackets: one forms a B in positive space, the other a G in negative space. It can be stamped, laser-etched, embroidered or printed in one colour. The wordmark is uppercase but compact, with custom spacing and no wide tech tracking.

### Favicon concept

The bracket intersection alone, reduced to a bold 2 × 2 modular form. Default is Carbon on Equipment White; selected application may use Ochre on Carbon.

### Icon style

- Solid and outline hybrid, chosen for recognition
- 2 px stroke, square ends
- 20 or 24 px base sizes
- Technical pictogram logic
- No illustrations disguised as icons
- All controls retain a text label where ambiguity is possible

### Button style

- 0 px radius
- Compact 48 px default height
- Primary Carbon with a 4 px ochre left indicator
- Secondary transparent with Carbon border
- Tertiary appears as an indexed text command: `03 / View expertise`
- Hover changes indicator width or fill; no shadow or vertical movement
- Focus is a double ring: Equipment White gap plus Carbon/Ochre outer line

### Cards

Cards are **field records**:

- Square border
- Number, status and category in a top metadata rail
- Dense but readable internal spacing
- Optional ochre status tab
- Content remains left aligned
- No image overlay text

### Links

Links are explicit commands with an index or trailing rule. Body links use a robust underline. Inline arrows are avoided unless they communicate direction or progression.

### Forms

- Equipment White fields on Calibration Grey
- Dark 1 px boundary; 2 px focus boundary
- Labels in Roboto Mono
- Clear numeric ordering for longer enquiries
- Field states presented like inspection status: `Required`, `Complete`, `Check`
- The brand remains polite; “field” language is visual, not gimmicky copy

### Illustration and imagery style

- Calibration grids
- Exploded views
- Cutaway diagrams
- Test results, before/after process maps and annotated interfaces
- Ochre highlights the variable being changed
- Teal identifies verified systems or completed stages
- No decorative pseudo-scientific graphics

### Photography style

- Close, tactile detail of equipment, materials and hands-on work
- Controlled flash or neutral daylight where it improves factual clarity
- Serial numbers, surfaces, fasteners, cables, screens and documents may remain visible
- Images can be cropped into strict panels with technical captions
- People shown as competent participants, never “corporate collaboration” models

### Motion principles

- Immediate and mechanical
- 100–180 ms for controls; up to 260 ms for larger disclosures
- Stepped progress and indexed reveals are acceptable
- No spring physics or soft floating
- Loading and process states communicate exact status in text

### Overall feeling

A technically capable practice that arrives, diagnoses, makes decisions and leaves a working result.

### Why it matches Bear Grid

Field Standard connects strongly to Bear Grid’s deep-tech origin and delivery-led offer. It is ownable and practical across digital and physical applications. It is the most forceful route and would work well for industrial audiences, but it carries a higher risk of feeling too operational for executive advisory and investor work. Careful typography and photography would be essential.

# Logo programme

## 10. Logo design criteria

Before any final artwork, every route should be tested against the same criteria:

- Works at 16 px without interior collapse
- Works in one colour
- Works reversed from a dark surface
- Has no dependence on a rounded-square app container
- Can be printed, stamped, engraved and embroidered
- Feels credible next to engineering, investment and professional-services peers
- Is not mistaken for an outdoor, gaming, security or children’s brand
- Has a clear relationship to both “Bear” and “Grid” without illustrating both literally
- Pairs with a wordmark and can also stand alone
- Avoids hidden complexity that requires explanation to be appreciated

## 11. Logo concept 1 — Refined Bear / The Sentinel

**Type:** Refined bear mark + wordmark

The existing bear is redrawn as a frontal, highly reduced silhouette. The ears, brow and muzzle are defined by five or six broad geometric decisions rather than facial details. The expression is neutral and attentive—not friendly, aggressive or cartoon-like.

The lower edge follows a horizontal datum, giving the mark a stable architectural base. A subtle central negative-space line can suggest precision or alignment.

**Strengths**

- Preserves brand recognition and heritage
- Emotionally memorable without becoming a mascot
- Suitable as a secondary seal or founder mark

**Risks**

- Even a refined animal symbol can pull the identity toward outdoor equipment or security
- Requires exceptional restraint to avoid “logo bear” clichés

**Best fit:** Engineered Quiet as a secondary heritage mark; Civic Modern as a primary mark if the abstraction is strong enough.

Broad silhouette; central negative datum; no eyes or smile in final form.

## 12. Logo concept 2 — Geometric Bear / Nine-Cell Bear

**Type:** Geometric bear + abstract grid

A bear head emerges from a 3 × 3 matrix. Selected modules define the ears and outer head, while negative space creates the muzzle. The grid can remain implied in the primary mark and become explicit in diagrams or motion.

**Strengths**

- Unites the name’s two words in one construction
- Performs well as a system generator
- Offers a clear favicon reduction

**Risks**

- Literal grid modules can feel digital or blockchain-like
- Small optical corrections are needed so it does not look pixel-art or gaming-related

**Best fit:** Civic Modern.

```text
■ □ ■
■ ■ ■
□ ■ □
The final drawing would use optical curves or cut corners, not literal pixel blocks.
```

## 13. Logo concept 3 — Engineering Symbol / Datum Assembly

**Type:** Engineering-inspired symbol

Two perpendicular datum lines meet at a controlled offset. The negative space around the joint produces the suggestion of a B on the left and a G on the right. One small square identifies the reference point.

This is not a generic crosshair: the proportions are derived from the wordmark’s cap height and stroke.

**Strengths**

- Strong alignment with engineering and decision-making
- Excellent small-size and one-colour performance
- Generates a complete graphic language for layouts and diagrams

**Risks**

- Needs a distinctive silhouette to avoid becoming an anonymous technical glyph
- The bear association is indirect

**Best fit:** Engineered Quiet.

```text
      ┃
  ━━━━╋━━□
      ┃
Datum lines establish origin, alignment and a critical point.
```

## 14. Logo concept 4 — Abstract Grid / Interlock

**Type:** Abstract grid symbol

Two square brackets interlock. The left bracket and central bar imply B; the right bracket and open terminal imply G. The open area communicates a problem space; the interlock communicates integration and delivery.

**Strengths**

- Highly reproducible on screen and physical objects
- Compact, strong and non-illustrative
- Connects “grid” to structured problem solving

**Risks**

- Bracket marks are common in technology and architecture
- Must avoid looking like a scanning, coding or cybersecurity icon

**Best fit:** Field Standard.

```text
┌─┐┌
│ ├┘
└─┘
Interlocking positive and negative forms; final proportions remain square.
```

## 15. Logo concept 5 — BG Datum Monogram

**Type:** Monogram

A compact BG monogram constructed on a 6 × 6 unit grid. The B uses one vertical spine and two open bowls; the G is cut from the lower-right area. The shared stroke prevents the mark becoming decorative initials. A datum square can act as a controlled accent.

**Strengths**

- Ownable, professional and highly adaptable
- Links naturally to the brand name without literal illustration
- Performs best as favicon, document seal and social avatar
- Supports a premium consulting posture

**Risks**

- Many BG monograms exist; the construction and negative space must be genuinely specific
- Requires trademark screening before final selection

**Best fit:** Engineered Quiet; recommended primary route.

```text
╔══╗
║╦═╝
║╚═·
╚══╝
Shared spine, open G terminal, one datum point.
```

## 16. Logo concept 6 — Precision Wordmark

**Type:** Wordmark

“Bear Grid” is drawn as a restrained custom grotesk wordmark. The most distinctive gesture is the relationship between the **G** and **r**: the G’s open terminal aligns with the horizontal of the r, creating a visible internal grid line. The B receives a slightly squared lower bowl; the i dot becomes a small datum square.

Sentence case is preferred. Uppercase may be retained only if testing shows that it does not become overly industrial.

**Strengths**

- Mature, confident and consultancy-first
- Avoids all mascot and technology-symbol clichés
- Puts the unusual name at the centre of recognition

**Risks**

- Needs a separate favicon or micro-mark
- Distinctiveness depends on excellent letterform work

**Best fit:** All three directions; especially Civic Modern.

```text
Bear Gr·d
     └── aligned G terminal and square i datum
```

## 17. Logo concept 7 — Combination Mark / Bear Datum

**Type:** Combination mark

A compact geometric bear silhouette is bisected by a vertical datum line. The line continues into the wordmark’s baseline and becomes a system element in layouts. The symbol and name can separate, but their alignment always follows the same datum.

**Strengths**

- Balances heritage, recognisability and engineering logic
- Creates a distinctive horizontal signature for headers and reports
- Offers both emotional and rational brand cues

**Risks**

- More complex than the monogram or wordmark routes
- Must not resemble a heraldic crest or security badge

**Best fit:** Engineered Quiet or Civic Modern.

```text
[ BEAR│DATUM ]──────── BEAR GRID
      │
      Shared line continues into the layout system.
```

## 18. Logo shortlist recommendation

Advance three concepts to rough black-and-white sketches:

1. **BG Datum Monogram** — strongest primary recommendation
2. **Precision Wordmark** — essential companion and viable standalone route
3. **Refined Bear / The Sentinel** — heritage and recognisability test

The Abstract Grid / Interlock may be retained as a reserve route if a more industrial expression is preferred.

No colour, animation or polished presentation should be used in the first logo review. The decision should be based on silhouette, proportion, meaning and reproduction.

# Design system foundations

## 19. Recommended token framework

The tokens below are the proposed shared foundation for all directions. Colour and typography values change by direction; geometry and accessibility should remain stable.

### Spacing scale

Use a 4 px base with a deliberate 8 px working rhythm.

| Token | Value | Typical use |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `space-0` | 0 | Reset |
| `space-1` | 4 px | Icon/detail gap |
| `space-2` | 8 px | Compact internal gap |
| `space-3` | 12 px | Label-to-control gap |
| `space-4` | 16 px | Base content gap |
| `space-5` | 24 px | Component padding |
| `space-6` | 32 px | Card/module padding |
| `space-7` | 48 px | Subsection gap |
| `space-8` | 64 px | Mobile section gap |
| `space-9` | 80 px | Compact desktop section gap |
| `space-10` | 96 px | Standard desktop section gap |
| `space-11` | 128 px | Major section gap |
| `space-12` | 160 px | Hero / exceptional separation |

Guidance:

- Standard desktop sections: 96–128 px vertical padding
- Mobile sections: 64–80 px vertical padding
- Avoid 140 px as an unexamined default
- Maintain 24–32 px readable internal padding for modules
- Use large empty space only when it separates distinct arguments

### Border radius

| Token | Value | Use |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `radius-0` | 0 px | Ruled modules, image frames |
| `radius-1` | 2 px | Buttons, fields in Engineered Quiet |
| `radius-2` | 4 px | Civic controls and cards |
| `radius-round` | 999 px | Status dots or externally mandated brand controls only |

Rounded pills should not be a core Bear Grid shape.

### Borders

| Token | Value |
| --- | --- |
| `rule-hairline` | 1 px solid semantic rule colour |
| `rule-strong` | 1 px solid semantic strong-rule colour |
| `rule-active` | 2 px solid signature colour |
| `focus-ring` | 2 px solid signature/high-contrast focus colour, 2 px offset |

### Shadows

The identity is predominantly flat.

| Token | Value | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `shadow-none` | none | Default |
| `shadow-overlay` | `0 12px 36px rgba(19, 20, 18, 0.14)` | Menus, consent, modal surfaces only |
| `shadow-dialog` | `0 24px 72px rgba(19, 20, 18, 0.20)` | Rare major dialog |

Do not use shadows on service cards, buttons, partner marks or standard sections.

## 20. Grid

### Desktop

- 12 columns
- Maximum content grid: 1280 px
- Outer margin: 32 px minimum, growing fluidly
- Gutter: 24 px at 1024–1279 px; 32 px from 1280 px
- Primary editorial split: 4 columns / 8 columns
- Hero option: 8 columns / 4 columns
- Long-form text: 6–7 columns maximum

### Tablet

- 8 columns
- 24 px outer margin
- 20–24 px gutter
- Major splits collapse to 3 / 5 before becoming single column

### Mobile

- 4 columns
- 20 px outer margin, optionally 24 px above 390 px
- 16 px gutter
- Most content spans all four columns
- Labels may occupy one column only when the remaining three remain readable

### Baseline rhythm

- 8 px baseline for layout
- 4 px optical correction allowed inside type and icons
- Rules, labels and diagrams should visibly align to column or baseline references

## 21. Container widths

| Container | Maximum |
| --- | ---: |
| Full editorial | 1280 px |
| Standard page | 1120 px |
| Contact / split page | 1120 px |
| Reading / policy | 760 px |
| Narrow statement | 680 px |
| Data table | 1280 px with controlled horizontal overflow on small screens |

The current 1320 px maximum can be reduced slightly to increase visual control and reading cohesion while preserving the architecture.

## 22. Heading hierarchy

Recommended values use fluid sizing with capped desktop maxima.

| Style | Desktop | Mobile | Weight | Line height | Tracking |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Display | 72–88 px | 48–56 px | 500–550 | 0.94–0.98 | `-0.035em` |
| H1 | 56–72 px | 42–50 px | 500–600 | 0.98–1.02 | `-0.03em` |
| H2 | 40–52 px | 32–38 px | 500–600 | 1.02–1.08 | `-0.025em` |
| H3 | 26–32 px | 24–28 px | 550–650 | 1.12–1.2 | `-0.015em` |
| H4 | 20–22 px | 19–21 px | 600 | 1.25 | `-0.005em` |
| Eyebrow | 12–13 px | 12 px | 600 | 1.35 | `0.08em` |
| Technical label | 12–13 px mono | 12 px mono | 500 | 1.4 | `0.04em` |

Avoid 100+ px headlines as a default. They can be used once in a controlled campaign or printed application, not throughout the interface.

## 23. Body typography

| Style | Size | Line height | Measure |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Lead | 21–24 px | 1.45–1.55 | 38–52 characters |
| Body large | 18–20 px | 1.55–1.65 | 55–70 characters |
| Body | 16–18 px | 1.6–1.7 | 55–75 characters |
| Small | 14–15 px | 1.5–1.6 | 60–80 characters |
| Caption | 12–13 px | 1.45–1.55 | Flexible |

Body copy should never use compressed line height to achieve a more “designed” look.

## 24. Buttons and control sizes

| Size | Height | Horizontal padding | Text |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Small | 40 px | 14–16 px | 13–14 px |
| Default | 48 px | 18–22 px | 14–15 px |
| Large | 56 px | 24–28 px | 15–16 px |
| Icon-only | 44 × 44 px minimum | — | Accessible name required |

Other control requirements:

- Inputs: 52 px minimum height
- Textareas: 144 px minimum height
- Mobile menu links: 48 px minimum tap height
- Disclosure summaries: 52 px minimum
- Checkbox/radio visual: 20–24 px with a 44 px interactive area

## 25. Component principles

### Buttons

Use one strong primary action per section. Secondary actions should not compete through equal fill. Avoid button clusters unless choices are genuinely parallel.

### Links

Underlines are the default accessibility cue in body text. Do not remove them solely for visual cleanliness. Navigation can use a separate active marker.

### Forms

Form controls must have persistent, visible boundaries. Labels never disappear into placeholders. Error states must state what happened and how to resolve it.

### Cards

Prefer shared-border grids, lists and ruled records. Use a floating card only when the content is genuinely portable or layered.

### Icons

Do not add an icon when a two-word label is clearer. Create a small custom set only after the chosen identity direction is approved.

### Partner marks

- Normalise each mark inside the same optical bounding box
- Use one-colour partner artwork when contractual guidance allows
- Otherwise retain original colour on a consistent neutral field
- Never stretch or force equal physical width
- Separate “partners,” “community,” “clients” and “memberships” semantically
- Keep the current disclaimer

## 26. Imagery and diagram system

Every image should serve one of four purposes:

1. **Context** — where the work happens
2. **Evidence** — what was built, tested or learned
3. **People** — who is responsible for the work
4. **Explanation** — how a system, process or decision works

If an image does none of these, do not use it.

Suggested reusable formats:

- Wide environment + narrow evidence detail
- Contact sheet of 3–6 factual project images
- Annotated system plate
- Before / after process map
- Decision tree or constraint diagram
- Historical artefact on a neutral archival mat

## 27. Accessibility and quality gates

Before any future rollout:

- WCAG 2.2 AA contrast audit for every semantic colour pair
- Keyboard review of header, navigation, forms, accordions, consent and utilities
- 200% zoom and reflow test at 320 CSS px
- Reduced-motion verification
- Screen-reader check of headings, landmark order, labels, errors and partner marks
- Favicon test at 16, 20, 32, 48 and 180 px
- Logo test in black, reversed white, one spot colour and low-quality office printing
- Typography test on macOS, Windows, iOS and Android fallbacks
- Long-title and localisation stress test
- Print test for proposals and A4 reports

# Approval and rollout sequence

## 31. One-by-one visual review

No implementation should begin from this document alone. Visuals should be presented in chat in the following approval gates, one set at a time.

### Approval gate 1 — Identity direction

Present one compact visual board for each direction:

- Colour swatches
- Type hierarchy specimen
- One homepage crop
- One document/report crop
- One image or diagram treatment

**Decision required:** Select one direction, or explicitly authorise a hybrid.

### Approval gate 2 — Logo concepts

Present the three shortlisted logo routes in black and white:

- Primary mark
- Wordmark pairing
- 16 px favicon test
- Reversed version
- A4 report-cover application

**Decision required:** Select one route and identify any required refinements.

### Approval gate 3 — Core system

Present:

- Final palette
- Type scale
- Button, link and form states
- Service module
- Partner-mark treatment
- Diagram sample

**Decision required:** Approve the design language before page compositions.

### Approval gate 4 — Key page visuals

Present one at a time:

1. Homepage desktop
2. Homepage mobile
3. Expertise page
4. Investors page
5. History page and original-platform transition
6. Contact page
7. Header, footer, consent and utility controls

**Decision required:** Approve each visual before any implementation.

### Approval gate 5 — Identity production

Only after explicit approval:

- Draw final logo artwork
- Prepare favicon and app icons
- Produce master colour and type tokens
- Create diagram and icon masters
- Prepare partner-logo assets
- Document accessibility pairs and usage rules

### Approval gate 6 — Website implementation

Implementation is a separate authorised phase. It should preserve the current architecture and positioning, replace visual tokens and components systematically, and include accessibility and responsive verification.
